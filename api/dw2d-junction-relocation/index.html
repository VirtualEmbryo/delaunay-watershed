    Junction relocation and interface projection (2D)¶

    Move a finished 2D mesh's junction vertices onto the junctions the mask itself gives (opt-in).

    Opt-in. It is the 2D port of dw3d's junction_relocation + junction_curves: the connectivity is frozen (the edge array, hence every interface and junction identity, is never changed); only the coordinates of junction vertices move, and only when four guards accept the move.

    Why¶

    dw2d's mesh vertices are Delaunay points chosen on the pixel lattice (integer row, column), so every junction vertex sits on a pixel centre, while a junction of the label image lies on the dual lattice: the corner shared by four pixels. On the equilibrium-foam benchmark (exact rasterisations of certified circular-arc foams with known tensions; held-out split, 159 images, min_dist 3) moving the junctions onto the mask's own junctions lowered the case angle error (per case the median over junctions of the mean absolute error of the three material-attributed angles, then the median over cases) by 1.76 deg [1.51, 1.94] from 9.15 deg, the junction position error by 0.21 px, and the gauge-matched tension error by 0.025 [0.011, 0.029] (per-junction Young-Dupre rows), on every scale tier (cell radius 10, 20, 40 px). With smooth boundary noise of 0.5 px RMS it still helps (-1.49 deg, -0.019); at 1 px it helps the angles only (-0.66 deg); at 2 px it is neutral on angles. These are measurements on synthetic masks, not a guarantee on real masks.

    What it does¶

    • Mask junctions. A dual-lattice corner at continuous (row, col) = (i + 0.5, j + 0.5) is shared by pixels (i, j), (i, j+1), (i+1, j), (i+1, j+1). It is a junction corner of the triple (a, b, c) when its four pixels hold exactly those three labels; a corner whose four pixels hold four labels counts for each of the four triples it contains (dw3d's rule, which carries a junction into a four-fold point instead of stopping it short). Per triple, 8-connected components of junction corners are one mask junction each, located at the component's centroid.
    • Identity. Mesh junctions (vertices whose incident edges involve exactly three materials) are paired with mask junctions of the same triple by the Hungarian assignment on Euclidean distance, pairs farther than gate_px (3 px) rejected, and only for triples whose two counts agree ("matched"). A vertex of four or more materials is never moved.
    • Guards. Moves are applied one vertex at a time in ascending vertex index against the configuration as it stands. A move is accepted only if (1) no incident edge becomes shorter than MIN_EDGE_PX; (2) the number of proper crossings between non-adjacent edges does not increase — moving v changes only the edges incident to v, so the change of the whole-mesh count equals the change restricted to pairs involving those edges (an exact identity, evaluated against a candidate set that provably contains every edge that can meet them); (3) no edge incident to the moved vertex comes into a contact it was not in before the move (see "Edge contacts" below); (4) no material's signed area (dw2d Dcel.compute_areas over the oriented edges) changes sign or reaches zero. A refused move is dropped, never repaired: the vertex stays where it was. Whole-mesh recounts of crossings and of contacts before and after certify (2) and (3) globally; a rise raises.

    Edge contacts¶

    Guard (2) counts proper crossings in floating point, so it lets through a move that makes two edges touch: on the equilibrium-foam benchmark at a cell radius of 40 px, the projection of :mod:dw2d.locus_projection flattened 141 Z-shaped turns of plain interface chains so that a vertex landed exactly on a non-adjacent edge (an endpoint inside another edge) and the edge before it folded back by exactly 180 degrees. Guard (3) refuses every new contact of either kind, decided exactly:

    • two edges sharing no vertex are in contact when the closed segments meet: a proper crossing, a T-touch (an endpoint of one lies on the other), coincident vertices (two distinct vertices with identical coordinates, endpoints of both), or a collinear overlap (four collinear endpoints sharing one point or a sub-segment);
    • two edges sharing one vertex s are in contact when their other endpoints lie on the same ray from s: one edge runs back along the other (an adjacent overlap, a turn of exactly 180 degrees).

    A pair counts as new when it is not in contact before the move, or in contact of another class. Every coordinate is a binary64 float, hence an exact rational. Orientation signs use Shewchuk's static filter for orient2d and, when the filter cannot certify the sign, the determinant in fractions.Fraction; comparisons of coordinates are float comparisons, which are exact. On that benchmark the guard refuses 143 projection moves in 38 of the 53 meshes at 40 px and leaves 0 contacts; at 10 and 20 px, where the projection creates none, it refuses nothing and the vertices are bitwise unchanged. It keeps the Z turns of the plain mesh (two turns of opposite signs) rather than removing them.

    Units and conventions: coordinates in pixels, (row, col), the frame of geometry_reconstruction_2d's vertices; the label image is the one the mesh was built from (after expansion_labels). Nothing reads ground truth. Deterministic.

    Failure modes: a mesh with no matched triple is returned unchanged (planned = 0); a triple whose mesh and mask junction counts differ is left untouched and counted (unmatched_triples); the thresholds are fixed and are not tuning parameters.

    GuardedVertexMover ¶

    GuardedVertexMover(verts, edges)

    Vertex positions over a frozen edge array, moved one vertex at a time under the four guards.

    Inputs: verts (V, 2) float (copied), edges (E, 4) int [v1, v2, m1, m2]. try_move(v, p) applies the move and returns "" or the name of the refusing guard, checked in this order: "short_edge", "crossing", "contact", "area_sign". The candidate search for the crossing and contact guards is a KD-tree on the initial edge midpoints; its radius adds twice the largest displacement applied so far, which bounds both how far any midpoint has moved and how much any half-length has grown, so the candidate set always contains every edge the moved edges can meet.

    whole_mesh_contacts ¶

    whole_mesh_contacts(verts, edges)

    Number of pairs of edges of a 2D mesh in contact, of any class (see "Edge contacts" above).

    verts (V, 2) float, edges (E, >= 2) int (first two columns = vertex indices). Pairs of edges sharing no vertex count when the closed segments meet (proper crossing, T-touch, coincident vertices, collinear overlap); pairs sharing one vertex count when one runs back along the other. Zero-length edges are skipped. Candidate pairs come from a KD-tree on edge midpoints with a radius covering the two longest half-edges, then an exact bounding-box test; the classes are exact.

    whole_mesh_crossings ¶

    whole_mesh_crossings(verts, edges)

    Number of proper crossings between non-adjacent edges of a 2D mesh.

    verts (V, 2) float, edges (E, >= 2) int (first two columns = vertex indices). Candidate pairs come from a KD-tree on edge midpoints with a radius covering the two longest half-edges, so no crossing can be missed.

    signed_areas ¶

    signed_areas(verts, edges)

    {material: signed area} from dw2d's oriented edge array (the formula of Dcel.compute_areas).

    On dw2d meshes every cell's value has the same sign (about its pixel count, positive since 0.8.0, when each edge became the positively oriented boundary of its first material; negative before); the area guard relies only on that consistency, which tests/test_junction_relocation.py pins.

    Move a 2D mesh's interface vertices onto the mask's own interface locus (opt-in, after the junction relocation).

    The 2D port of dw3d's locus projection. Connectivity is frozen: only the coordinates of interface vertices move, under the guards of :mod:dw2d.junction_relocation.

    Why, and why only after the junction relocation¶

    dw2d's vertices are pixel centres, while the label image's interface between labels a and b lies between pixel centres. On the equilibrium-foam benchmark (exact rasterisations of certified circular-arc foams with known tensions; held-out split, 159 images, min_dist 3), the projection alone made the case angle error worse (+0.46 deg [+0.19, +0.56]) and the tension error worse (+0.014), but applied after the junction relocation it lowered the angle error by 2.45 deg [2.07, 2.68] from 9.15 deg and the gauge-matched tension error by 0.029, and it stayed better than the legacy mesh with smooth boundary noise of 0.5 and 1 px RMS (-1.96 deg and -1.29 deg; tensions -0.023 and -0.021). The interaction (harmful alone, helpful composed) is measured, its mechanism is not. Hence geometry_reconstruction_2d(..., project_interface_vertices=True) requires relocate_junctions=True.

    What it does¶

    For every vertex of exactly one interface (a, b) — degree 2, both incident edges carrying the same pair, not a junction, not on the image border — the target is the zero of psi_ab(x) = (D_a(x) - D_b(x)) / 2, D_x the Euclidean distance to the nearest pixel centre of label x (scipy.ndimage.distance_transform_edt), interpolated bilinearly. Newton steps along grad psi, at most MAX_ITERATIONS, stop at |psi| < TOLERANCE_PX; a vertex whose iteration does not converge, or would move farther than MAX_DISPLACEMENT_PX, is left in place. Moves are applied in ascending vertex index under the four guards (short edge, crossing count, edge contact, signed area); whole-mesh recounts of crossings and of contacts before and after certify the crossing and contact guards globally (a rise raises). The contact guard is the one that matters here: on the equilibrium-foam benchmark at 40 px, the projection without it flattened 141 Z-shaped turns of plain interface chains into an edge touching another and a turn of exactly 180 degrees; with it those moves are refused and the vertex stays where it was (:mod:dw2d.junction_relocation, "Edge contacts").

    Units and conventions: pixels, (row, col), the frame of geometry_reconstruction_2d's vertices; the label image the mesh was built from (after expansion_labels). Nothing reads ground truth; deterministic. Cost: one distance transform per label present.
